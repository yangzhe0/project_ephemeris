1. Choosing a target

  • The ten preset buttons show the current altitude and observing window at the selected station. Green means at least 30% of the window remains, orange means less than 30%, blue means the window has yet to start, and gray means it has ended. Select a target to calculate its ephemeris and generate a finder chart.
  • The dropdown includes Jupiter’s Galilean moons (5001–5004), more moons of Saturn and Uranus, and planet centers. Choose Enter a satellite code to enter an MSU natural satellite code manually.

2. Observatory and night window

  • Choose a preset observatory, enter a three-character IAU/MPC station code, or select 500 for geocentric coordinates.
  • The header shows the selected station’s observing window in Beijing time (UTC+8). Hover over it for the astronomical darkness window and sunrise.
  • When MSU does not list a station, the tool requests geocentric coordinates and uses the selected station’s location to compute altitude and twilight.

3. Observation time

  • Leave the field blank to use the current time. Enter a specific time in Beijing time (UTC+8), for example 2026 11 05 21 30 00 or 20261105213000. The tool converts it to UTC for the ephemeris.

4. Reading and copying coordinates

  • Declination includes a sign, even between −1° and +1°. Right ascension and declination use two digits for hours or degrees and minutes. Click Copy coordinates to copy the values for a telescope controller.

5. Altitude and DSS finder charts

  • Green indicates altitude of at least 30°, amber indicates 0° up to 30°, and gray indicates below the horizon.
  • The STScI DSS red-band finder chart marks the target with a red crosshair. Open it fullscreen to zoom and pan, save the chart, or open the view in Stellarium.

Ephemerides are provided by SAI MSU. Finder charts use the STScI Digitized Sky Survey.

The clock in the header shows the current UTC time.
